Kotor to Ksamil: a 10-day beach road trip

The best of both coasts in one trip — fjord-like bays and walled towns in Montenegro, then the turquoise coves of the Albanian Riviera.

By Daniel · Updated 24 October 2026 · About 18 minutes to read

Photo: Bay of Kotor by Pudelek (Marcin Szala), CC BY-SA 3.0

This route covers roughly 450 km of coast and crosses one border. It is designed for a rental car, with no single day longer than about five hours of driving, and it puts you on a beach every day. Along the way you'll see two UNESCO-listed sites, one of the most spectacular mountain roads in the Balkans and some of the clearest water in the Mediterranean.

Prefer public transport? Every leg can also be done by bus, just more slowly — see the "without a car" variation below and our getting-there guide for options.

At a glance: fly into Tivat or Podgorica · 4 nights in Montenegro · 6 nights in Albania · fly home from Tirana, or take the ferry from Sarandë to Corfu and fly from there · best months: June and September.

Before you go

  • Book the car early for June to September, and tell the rental company you'll cross the border. You'll need their written authorisation and insurance valid in both countries.
  • Book the first and last nights in advance; in June and September you can book the rest a few days ahead. In July and August, book everything.
  • Carry both currencies: euros for Montenegro and lek for Albania. Withdraw lek at an ATM in Shkodër or Vlorë.
  • Download offline maps for the whole route — signal drops on the Llogara Pass and on smaller coastal roads.

Days 1–4: Montenegro

Day 1 · 20–30 min

Kotor and Perast

Land at Tivat — the airport is only about 20 minutes from Kotor. Drop your bags and walk into the old town through the Sea Gate. Kotor is a maze of marble lanes, churches and small squares; get lost on purpose, then climb the city walls to the Fortress of San Giovanni: around 1,350 steps, best done early morning or at sunset when it is cooler.

Kotor itself has no great beach, so in the afternoon drive 20 minutes around the bay to Perast, a tiny baroque town where you can take a boat out to the islet church of Our Lady of the Rocks and swim off the stone piers. Stay for sunset and dinner on the waterfront.

Sleep: inside or just outside Kotor's walls (Dobrota is quieter and cheaper). Tip: check the cruise ship schedule and avoid the old town between 10:00 and 16:00 on busy days. More in our Kotor travel guide.

Day 2 · 30–45 min

Budva: old town and Mogren

Take the road over the hill to Budva — or, if you started early, detour up the Kotor serpentine for the view and continue via Njeguši for a smoked-ham breakfast. In Budva, walk through the compact Old Town, with its citadel and churches, then follow the cliff path to Mogren Beach, two coves linked by a short rock passage.

If you want more space, drive 15 minutes west to Jaz Beach. Budva is the place for a night out — the seafront promenade is busy until late all summer.

Sleep: Budva or neighbouring Bečići. Tip: parking in central Budva is expensive and scarce; choose accommodation with parking.

Day 3 · 30 min

Sveti Stefan and Petrovac

Arrive at Sveti Stefan before 10:00 to get a spot on the public section of the beach, to the left of the isthmus when facing the island. The island itself is part of a private resort, but the view from the road above is free and classic. Walk the coastal path north towards Pržno for more coves.

Continue to Petrovac, a relaxed town with a pretty promenade and a small Venetian fortress, and spend the afternoon at Lučice, a pine-ringed cove a 10-minute walk south.

Sleep: Petrovac. Eat: grilled fish on the promenade, or a cheaper meal one street back.

Day 4 · 1 h 15 min

Ulcinj, Velika Plaža and Ada Bojana

Head south past Bar to Ulcinj, the most Albanian-influenced town in Montenegro, with minarets on the skyline and a fortified old town above the sea. Spend the day on Velika Plaža, the long sandy beach that's ideal for kids and kitesurfers; in the afternoon the wind picks up and the kites come out.

For dinner, drive to the Bojana river and eat grilled fish at one of the restaurants built on stilts over the water, then watch the sunset from Ada Bojana.

Sleep: Ulcinj. Tip: fill the tank and get some cash before tomorrow's border crossing.

Days 5–10: Albania

Day 5 · 4–5 h

Across the border to Vlorë

This is the long day, so start early. Cross at Sukobin–Muriqan, the closest crossing to Ulcinj. Have your passport or ID card, car registration, insurance and the rental company's cross-border authorisation ready. Outside summer weekends it usually takes 15–30 minutes.

Stop in Shkodër for coffee and the views from Rozafa Castle over the lake and rivers, and withdraw lek at an ATM. Then drive south on the main highway past Tirana and Durrës to Vlorë, the city where Albanian independence was declared in 1912. Break the drive with a swim on Vlorë's southern beaches in the late afternoon and a walk along the long seafront promenade.

Car hire warning: many rental companies charge high one-way fees for dropping a car in another country, or don't allow it at all. A common alternative is to return your Montenegrin car in Ulcinj or Podgorica, take a bus or transfer to Shkodër, and rent a new car in Albania.
Day 6 · 1 h 15 min

Llogara Pass and Dhërmi

The road south of Vlorë follows the coast past Orikum, then climbs through pine forest to the Llogara Pass, at just over 1,000 m. On the other side it zig-zags down with one of the best coastal views in the Balkans: on a clear day you can see Corfu. Stop at the viewpoints and the cafés in the forest, where roast lamb and local honey are specialities, but stay alert — the hairpins are tight and buses use the road.

Settle in at Dhërmi, where the long white-pebble beach is lined with beach clubs. Walk up to the old hillside village in the evening for sunset views and a quieter dinner.

Sleep: Dhërmi, on the beach or up in the village.

Day 7 · 30 min

Gjipe and Jale

Start early for Gjipe: park above the beach and walk down for 20–30 minutes on a rough track. Wear proper shoes and carry plenty of water, because the walk back up is hot and exposed. At the beach, explore the mouth of the canyon behind it.

In the afternoon, move on to Jale for snorkelling and a sunset drink, then continue to Himarë, a real working town with a good choice of restaurants, a small harbour and a ruined hilltop castle.

Sleep: Himarë. Tip: boats from Himarë also run to Gjipe and other coves if you'd rather skip the hike.

Day 8 · 1 h 45 min

Himarë to Ksamil, with three great stops

Have a morning swim at Livadhi Beach in Himarë, then drive south on the coastal road. Stop at Porto Palermo to visit the small triangular castle and swim in the calm bay. Further south, take a break in Qeparo, whose old village clings to the hillside above the new one, and again at Borsh, where the beach is so long you'll always find space.

Arrive in Ksamil for the evening and eat at one of the small seafood restaurants by the water.

Sleep: Ksamil — or Sarandë, which is cheaper and livelier in the evenings.

Day 9 · about 1 h 30 min in total

Ksamil, Butrint and the Blue Eye

Hit Ksamil's beaches at 08:00, before the day-trippers arrive, and swim or paddle out to the islets. Around midday, drive 10 minutes to Butrint National Park, a UNESCO site with Greek, Roman, Byzantine and Venetian ruins set among lagoons and woods. Allow two to three hours, and bring water and a hat.

In the late afternoon, head inland to the Blue Eye, a spring where intensely blue water wells up from a deep pool, about 45 minutes from Sarandë. Return to Ksamil for a final seafood dinner. Not sure about Ksamil? Read is Ksamil worth it?

Day 10

Heading home

You have two options. You can take the ferry from Sarandë to Corfu (around 30–70 minutes depending on the boat) and fly home from Corfu, which has many summer routes to Europe. Or you can drive about 4–5 hours back to Tirana International Airport, stopping for lunch in Gjirokastër on the way. With a spare day, spend it in the capital with our Tirana in 2 days guide.

If you have extra days, add Gjirokastër as an overnight stop, or Berat on the way back to Tirana.

Variations

  • 7 days: skip Ulcinj and Vlorë. Go Kotor → Budva → Sveti Stefan, then fly or take a bus from Podgorica to Tirana and spend 4 nights between Himarë and Ksamil.
  • 14 days: add a night in Perast, two nights at Durmitor National Park (rafting the Tara Canyon), and two extra nights on the Riviera with Gjirokastër and Berat. See our Montenegro and Albania guides for ideas.
  • Without a car: buses run along the Montenegrin coast all year, and between Ulcinj and Shkodër in summer. In Albania, buses link Tirana with Vlorë, Himarë and Sarandë. Base yourself in Budva, then Himarë and Sarandë, and use boat taxis to reach the coves.
  • Reverse direction: fly into Corfu, take the ferry to Sarandë and drive north, flying home from Tivat or Podgorica. Often cheaper flights in early summer.

Packing checklist

  • Water shoes — most beaches are pebbly, and some have sea urchins
  • Snorkel and mask for Jale, Gjipe and Ksamil
  • Reef-safe sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses
  • Comfortable shoes for the Kotor walls and the Gjipe track
  • A light jacket for the Llogara Pass and evenings in the mountains
  • Cash in both euros and Albanian lek for small beach bars
  • Offline maps, because signal drops on mountain roads
  • Car documents and the rental company's cross-border authorisation
  • A refillable water bottle with a filter

Road trip FAQ

Is it safe to drive in Montenegro and Albania?

Yes, with care. Main roads are in good condition, but mountain roads are narrow and winding, and local driving can be unpredictable. Avoid driving at night on unfamiliar roads, keep to the speed limits and take your time on the Llogara Pass.

How long does the border crossing take?

Usually 15–30 minutes outside peak season. On July and August weekends, queues of an hour or more are common. Crossing early in the morning helps.

Can I do this trip in winter?

You can drive the route, but many beach hotels and restaurants close from November to April, and the sea is too cold for most people. Late May to early October is the best window.
