Kotor to Ksamil: a 10-day beach road trip

The best of both coasts in one trip — fjord-like bays and walled towns in Montenegro, then the turquoise coves of the Albanian Riviera.

Updated 19 October 2026 · About 12 minutes to read

Photo: Bay of Kotor by Pudelek (Marcin Szala), CC BY-SA 3.0

This route covers roughly 450 km of coast and crosses one border. It is designed for a rental car, with no single day longer than about five hours of driving, and it puts you on a beach every day. Prefer public transport? Each leg can also be done by bus — see our getting-there guide for options.

At a glance: fly into Tivat or Podgorica · 4 nights in Montenegro · 6 nights in Albania · fly home from Tirana, or take the ferry from Sarandë to Corfu and fly from there.

Drive times are without stops and in normal traffic. Add 30–50% on July–August weekends, especially around Budva and at the border.

Days 1–4: Montenegro

Day 1 · 20–30 min

Kotor and Perast

Land at Tivat — the airport is only about 20 minutes from Kotor. Drop your bags and climb the city walls to the Fortress of San Giovanni: around 1,350 steps, best done early morning or at sunset when it is cooler. Kotor itself has no great beach, so in the afternoon drive 20 minutes around the bay to Perast, a tiny baroque town where you can take a boat out to the islet church of Our Lady of the Rocks and swim off the stone piers.

Day 2 · 30–45 min

Budva: old town and Mogren

Take the road over the hill to Budva. Walk through the compact Old Town, then follow the cliff path to Mogren Beach, two coves linked by a short rock passage. If you want more space, drive 15 minutes west to Jaz Beach. Budva is the place for a night out — the seafront promenade is busy until late all summer.

Day 3 · 30 min

Sveti Stefan and Petrovac

Arrive at Sveti Stefan before 10 am to get a spot on the public section of the beach, to the left of the isthmus when facing the island. The island itself is part of a private resort. Continue to Petrovac, a relaxed town with a pretty promenade, and spend the afternoon at Lučice, a pine-ringed cove a 10-minute walk south.

Day 4 · 1 h 15 min

Ulcinj, Velika Plaža and Ada Bojana

Head south past Bar to Ulcinj, the most Albanian-influenced town in Montenegro. Spend the day on Velika Plaža, the long sandy beach that is ideal for kids and kitesurfers. For dinner, drive to the Bojana river and eat grilled fish at one of the restaurants built on stilts.

Days 5–10: Albania

Day 5 · 4–5 h

Across the border to Vlorë

This is the long day. Cross at Sukobin–Muriqan, the closest crossing to Ulcinj. Have your passport or ID card, car registration and the rental company's cross-border authorisation ready. Stop in Shkodër for coffee and Rozafa Castle, then drive south past Tirana and Durrës to Vlorë. Break the drive with a swim on Vlorë's southern beaches in the late afternoon.

Car hire warning: Many rental companies charge high one-way fees for dropping a car in another country, or don't allow it at all. A common alternative is to return your Montenegrin car in Ulcinj or Podgorica, take a bus or transfer to Shkodër, and rent a new car in Albania.
Day 6 · 1 h 15 min

Llogara Pass and Dhërmi

The road climbs through pine forest to the Llogara Pass, at just over 1,000 m, then zig-zags down with one of the best coastal views in the Balkans. Stop at the viewpoints, but stay alert — the hairpins are tight and buses use the road. Settle in at Dhërmi, where the white-pebble beach is lined with beach clubs.

Day 7 · 30 min

Gjipe and Jale

Start early for Gjipe: park above the beach and walk down for 20–30 minutes on a rough track. Wear proper shoes and carry water, because the walk back up is hot. In the afternoon, move on to Jale for snorkelling and a sunset drink, then sleep in Himarë.

Day 8 · 1 h 45 min

Himarë to Ksamil, with two great stops

Have a morning swim at Livadhi Beach in Himarë, then drive south. Stop at Porto Palermo to visit the small castle and swim in the calm bay, and again at Borsh, where the beach is so long you'll always find space. Arrive in Ksamil for the evening.

Day 9 · about 1 h 30 min in total

Ksamil, Butrint and the Blue Eye

Hit Ksamil's beaches at 8 am, before the day-trippers arrive, and swim or paddle out to the islets. Around midday, drive 10 minutes to Butrint National Park, a UNESCO site with Greek, Roman and Venetian ruins. In the late afternoon, head inland to the Blue Eye, a spring with impossibly blue water that is about 45 minutes from Sarandë.

Day 10

Heading home

You have two options. You can take the ferry from Sarandë to Corfu (around 30–70 minutes depending on the boat) and fly home from Corfu, which has many summer routes to Europe. Or you can drive about 4–5 hours back to Tirana International Airport. If you have extra days, add Gjirokastër, a UNESCO-listed stone town about an hour inland.

Variations

  • 7 days: skip Ulcinj and Vlorë. Go Kotor → Budva → Sveti Stefan, then fly or drive from Podgorica to Tirana and spend 4 nights between Himarë and Ksamil.
  • 14 days: add a night in Perast, a night at Durmitor National Park, and two extra nights on the Riviera with Gjirokastër and Berat.
  • Without a car: buses run along the Montenegrin coast and down the Albanian Riviera in summer. Base yourself in Budva and then Himarë and Sarandë, and use boat taxis to reach the coves.

Packing checklist

  • Water shoes — most beaches are pebbly, and some have sea urchins
  • Snorkel and mask for Jale, Gjipe and Ksamil
  • Reef-safe sunscreen and a hat
  • Cash in both euros and Albanian lek for small beach bars
  • Offline maps, because signal drops on mountain roads
  • Car documents and the rental company's cross-border authorisation
