Did you know?

Surprising facts about the Balkans

A few things that surprise almost every first-time visitor to Montenegro and Albania.

170,000+

Concrete bunkers

Albania's communist regime built bunkers across the country between the 1960s and 1980s. Today some are cafés, art spaces and even museums, like Bunk'Art in Tirana.

€

The euro, outside the EU

Montenegro uses the euro even though it isn't a member of the European Union or the eurozone. It adopted it unilaterally in the early 2000s.

~1,300 m

Europe's deepest canyon

The Tara River Canyon in Montenegro is often called the deepest in Europe. You can raft through it from late spring to early autumn.

Nod = no

Head gestures, reversed

Traditionally in Albania, a nod can mean "no" and a shake of the head "yes". Younger people often use Western gestures, so listen for the words too.

2 seas

Adriatic and Ionian

Albania's coast faces two seas. The Ionian side in the south is where you'll find the clearest, most turquoise water.

1 rock at a time

A man-made island

The islet of Our Lady of the Rocks near Perast was built by sailors dropping stones into the sea. Locals still do it every July in a ceremony called the Fašinada.
