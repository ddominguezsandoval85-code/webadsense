The Balkan beach showdown

Montenegro vs Albania: Which Has the Better Beaches?

Two neighbours, one stunning coastline split in half. We compare water, sand, prices, crowds and vibe so you can pick the right beach holiday — or do both.

Choose Montenegro

If you want jaw-dropping mountain-meets-sea scenery, walled old towns steps from the beach, smoother infrastructure and an easy, compact trip.

Choose Albania

If you want the clearest turquoise water, wilder and quieter coves, lower prices and a destination that still feels like a discovery.

Side by side

Head-to-head comparison

Both countries share the same sunshine and sea, but the beach experience is surprisingly different. Montenegro's coast is short and steep, packed with medieval towns; Albania's is longer and wilder, and its southern half faces the Ionian Sea, where the water is noticeably clearer.

"Edge" marks where one country has a clear advantage for a typical beach traveller. Your priorities may differ.

Montenegro: the good

  • Postcard-perfect old towns right on the water
  • Compact coast — several beaches in one day
  • Uses the euro; reliable services and transport
  • Great base for hiking in Lovćen and Durmitor

Montenegro: the trade-offs

  • Mostly pebble beaches, little natural sand
  • Heavy traffic and crowds around Budva in summer
  • Premium sunbeds can be pricey near Sveti Stefan
  • Limited parking in the Bay of Kotor

Albania: the good

  • Some of the clearest water in the Mediterranean
  • Excellent value for food, drink and rooms
  • Hidden coves reachable by short hikes or boat
  • Warm, famously generous hospitality

Albania: the trade-offs

  • Long, curvy drives between beach towns
  • Ksamil has become crowded and built-up
  • Uneven quality of beach clubs and rentals
  • Uses the lek; cash still matters in small places

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Are Albania's beaches better than Montenegro's?

For clear turquoise water and value for money, Albania's Ionian coast (Ksamil, Dhërmi, Jale, Gjipe) usually wins. For dramatic scenery, polished resorts and short distances between beaches and old towns, Montenegro has the edge.

Is Albania cheaper than Montenegro for a beach holiday?

Yes, in most cases. Food, drinks, sunbeds and mid-range accommodation are typically 20–40% cheaper in Albania, although prices in hotspots like Ksamil have risen quickly in recent years.

Can I visit both Montenegro and Albania in one trip?

Yes. The Sukobin–Muriqan and Han i Hotit crossings connect the two countries, and a 10–14 day trip from Kotor down to Ksamil is a popular route. Expect waits at the border on summer weekends. See our road-trip itinerary.

When is the best time for beaches in both countries?

June and September offer warm water, fewer crowds and lower prices. July and August are the hottest and busiest months, while May and October are pleasant but the sea is cooler.

Do Montenegro and Albania have sandy beaches?

Yes, but they are the exception. Montenegro's sandiest spots are Velika Plaža and Ada Bojana near Ulcinj. In Albania, the northern coast around Durrës and Shëngjin is sandy, while the Riviera is mostly white pebble.

Is it safe to travel to Montenegro and Albania?

Both are generally considered safe for tourists. Use normal precautions with valuables on busy beaches, and take extra care on narrow mountain roads, especially at night. Check your government's travel advice before you go.

Do I need a visa?

Neither country is in the Schengen Area. Citizens of the EU, UK, US, Canada and Australia, among others, can usually visit both visa-free for short stays, but rules change — always confirm with the official government source for your nationality.
