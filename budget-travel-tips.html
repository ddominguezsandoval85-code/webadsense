Budget travel

How to travel on a budget: 30 tips that actually work

Travelling cheaply isn't about sleeping in train stations. It's a handful of good habits that add up to hundreds of euros saved on every trip.

By Daniel · Updated 24 October 2026 · About 16 minutes to read

Photo: Clem Onojeghuo, CC0

The biggest costs of any trip are almost always the same four: getting there, a bed, food and getting around. Cut each one a little and a trip that looked out of reach suddenly fits the budget — without sleeping in train stations or skipping the experiences you travelled for.

These tips are grouped by those categories, from planning to paying, with a sample daily budget for the Balkans and a list of the most common budget traps at the end.

Start with a plan

Before searching for a single flight, decide three things: how much you can spend in total, how long you want to travel and what really matters to you on this trip. Divide the total (minus flights) by the number of days and you have a daily budget to compare destinations with. Someone who wants to eat out every night should save on the bed; someone who loves boutique hotels should look for cheaper food and transport. Spending where it matters to you, and saving everywhere else, is the whole secret.

Keep track as you go. A simple note on your phone or a free expense-tracking app shows you each evening whether you're on budget, so there are no nasty surprises on the last day.

Flights

  1. Be flexible with dates. Use the month or calendar view on flight search engines to see the cheapest days. Moving your trip by a day or two — or flying midweek instead of at the weekend — often saves more than any other trick.
  2. Be flexible with airports. Check every airport within a few hours of your destination, both at home and abroad. For the Albanian Riviera, flying into Corfu and taking the ferry to Sarandë is sometimes cheaper than flying to Tirana; for Montenegro, compare Tivat, Podgorica and Dubrovnik.
  3. Set price alerts and wait for a dip instead of booking on impulse. For summer trips, start watching prices in winter.
  4. Travel light. On budget airlines, a cabin bag or checked bag can cost more than the seat. Learn each airline's bag sizes and pack to fit the free allowance — for a beach trip, a small backpack is usually enough.
  5. Compare the total price, not the headline fare. Bags, seat selection, payment fees and the cost of getting to and from a remote airport all add up.
  6. Consider overland routes. Buses and trains between neighbouring countries are often far cheaper than a short flight, you save a night's accommodation on overnight routes and you see more on the way.

Accommodation

  1. Travel in the shoulder season. In the Mediterranean, June and September offer great weather and warm sea at much lower prices than July and August. Accommodation is often 30–50% cheaper.
  2. Book a place with a kitchen. Making your own breakfast and the odd dinner can halve your daily food spend, especially for families and groups.
  3. Try family-run guesthouses. In the Balkans, a room in a family guesthouse is often cheaper and more welcoming than a hotel — and breakfast may be homemade. Hosts are also the best source of local tips.
  4. Hostels aren't just dorms. Many have private rooms at a fraction of hotel prices, plus free walking tours, a shared kitchen and other travellers to share taxis and tours with.
  5. Stay longer in fewer places. Weekly rates are often discounted, cleaning fees are spread over more nights and you'll spend less on transport between bases.
  6. Sleep one street back. Rooms a ten-minute walk from the beach or old town are usually far cheaper than those with a view — and quieter at night.
  7. Read the cancellation policy. Flexible rates cost a little more but can save you the whole amount if plans change. For non-refundable bookings, make sure your travel insurance covers cancellation.

Food and drink

  1. Eat where locals eat. Walk two streets away from the main square or seafront and prices drop instantly. A restaurant full of locals at lunchtime is a good sign.
  2. Make lunch your main meal. Many restaurants offer set lunch menus or daily specials that are much cheaper than the same food at dinner.
  3. Learn the local street food. A slice of byrek in Albania or a burek in Montenegro makes a filling breakfast for very little. Bakeries are a budget traveller's best friend.
  4. Shop at markets and supermarkets for picnics on the beach: bread, cheese, tomatoes, fruit and olives cost little and taste great by the sea.
  5. Carry a refillable water bottle — with a filter where tap water isn't drinkable. Buying bottled water several times a day adds up quickly in a hot country.
  6. Watch out for "tourist menus" with photos and no prices. Always check the price before ordering fish or seafood sold by weight, and check the bill before paying.

Getting around

  1. Use local buses and minibuses. They cost a fraction of taxis and private transfers, and they're how locals travel. In Albania, shared furgons go almost everywhere.
  2. Only rent a car when it pays off — for remote areas, for families, or when splitting the cost between several people. For a city break, a car is just an expensive parking problem.
  3. Avoid one-way rental fees, especially across borders. Returning a car where you picked it up is usually much cheaper; plan a loop if you can.
  4. Walk. Most old towns are best seen on foot anyway, it's free, and you'll find places you'd miss from a car.
  5. Agree taxi fares in advance where meters aren't common, or use ride-hailing apps where they're available. Ask your accommodation what a fair price is.

Money

  1. Get a card with no foreign transaction fees. Many online banks and travel cards offer this for free, and the savings add up over a long trip.
  2. Always pay in the local currency. When a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, say no — this "dynamic currency conversion" uses a much worse exchange rate.
  3. Avoid airport exchange desks; withdraw from a bank ATM instead, and check for ATM fees. Taking out larger amounts less often reduces fixed fees.
  4. Buy a local SIM or a travel eSIM rather than paying roaming charges outside your home zone. Montenegro and Albania are outside the EU's free-roaming area.

Experiences

  1. Look for free activities: tip-based walking tours, hiking trails, public beaches, viewpoints, markets and museums' free-entry days. Some of the best travel moments cost nothing.
  2. Don't skip travel insurance. It's the one thing not to save on — a single medical bill abroad can cost more than the whole trip. Compare policies and check what's covered before you buy.

Common budget traps

  • Peak-season dates: travelling during school holidays or local festivals can double prices for the same trip.
  • "Free" upgrades and add-ons: airline extras, hotel parking, resort fees and sunbed "packages" often appear only at the end.
  • Eating on the main square: the view is lovely, but you pay for it — have a drink there instead of a full meal.
  • Exchanging cash at the border or airport: rates are usually poor. Use an ATM in town.
  • Overpacking the itinerary: moving every night means more transport costs and more expensive one-night stays.

Budget packing list

  • A carry-on-sized backpack that fits budget airline rules
  • A refillable bottle with a filter
  • A quick-dry towel and a light sarong (beach blanket, scarf and cover-up in one)
  • Snorkel and mask, so you don't have to rent them
  • A universal adapter and a power bank
  • A small first-aid kit with sunscreen and after-sun — they cost more at beach resorts
  • A reusable shopping bag for market picnics

Budget travel FAQ

Is Albania or Montenegro cheaper?

Albania is generally cheaper for food, accommodation and local transport. Montenegro costs a little more, but distances are shorter and buses are frequent. Both are cheaper than Croatia or Greece.

How much should I budget for a week in the Balkans?

As a rough guide, a budget traveller can manage on €250–400 for a week, and a mid-range traveller on €450–800, excluding flights. July and August will push you towards the top of these ranges.

Is it worth booking everything in advance?

Book flights, the first night and anything in peak season early. In the shoulder season, keeping later nights flexible lets you stay longer where you like it and grab last-minute deals.
